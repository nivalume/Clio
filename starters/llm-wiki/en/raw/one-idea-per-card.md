# One idea per card

> Example source written for Clio's llm-wiki starter to show how ingesting works. Delete it whenever you like.

The usual way to take notes while reading is by source: finish a book, write a reading note. The trouble is that such
notes are rarely used again. An idea sits among everything else from the same book, so when you need it somewhere
else you neither remember where it is nor can you take it out on its own.

The alternative is to write each idea on its own card. One card holds one idea, in your own words rather than copied
from the text. A card like that can be cited directly by other cards, and it can sit next to cards from other books to
form a new argument.

Links between cards matter more than categories. An idea is often related to several subjects at once, while a folder
can only put it in one place. When cards are organised by links, the same card can take part in any number of lines
of thought.

When writing a card, first give it a title that stands on its own, ideally a complete statement. That forces you to
settle the conclusion before writing the reasons.
